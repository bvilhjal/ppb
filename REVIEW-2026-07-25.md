# PPB Review — Idea, Theory, Implementation (2026-07-25)

**Scope:** the scientific idea (Witteveen within-ancestry evaluator → cross-ancestry
portability measurement), the theory (`docs/METHOD.md`, `docs/CROSS_ANCESTRY.md`,
`docs/OVERLAP.md`, `docs/LIMITATIONS.md`, `docs/REAL_DATA.md`), and the
implementation (`src/ppb/`, `tests/`, `experiments/`, `scripts/`, `results/`).

**Commit:** `011bdee`. **Predecessor:** [`REVIEW.md`](REVIEW.md) (commit `bd5d0d9`,
2026-07-18) — all of its findings were resolved; none is re-raised here. This
review covers the work that post-dates it (real-data path, `ppb.overlap`, results
registry, leaderboard) and re-examines the idea and theory at the current state.

**What was run:** `pytest -q` → **337 passed, 45 skipped, 1 xfailed** in 49.9 s
(Python 3.11.15, numpy 2.4.6, numba 0.66.0). `experiments/cross_ancestry.py`
reproduces the table in `docs/CROSS_ANCESTRY.md` digit for digit. Five additional
checks were written for this review (parameters given inline below): a score-density
and LD-strength sweep of the cross-ancestry harness, a closed-form attack on the
benchmark metric, a Monte-Carlo check of the finite-sample bias constant, a
palindrome test of the CLI path, and timing/call-count instrumentation.

---

## Verdict

The algebra is right, the code is disciplined, and the claims hygiene is better
than most published methods work. What does not hold up is the **novelty framing**
and the **validation coverage**: the core identity predates the paper PPB
attributes it to, and the cross-ancestry simulation cannot exhibit the failure
mode the project says is dominant.

**No finding invalidates any number PPB has published** — not the real-data
tables in `docs/REAL_DATA.md`, not the results registry, and not the
`CROSS_ANCESTRY.md` demonstration (which reproduces exactly). What several
findings do change is how those numbers should be *read*.

## Idea

### I1 (major) — the core identity is not Witteveen et al.'s, and the docs say it is

`docs/CROSS_ANCESTRY.md` §Background states:

> The idea originates with **Witteveen et al. (2022)**… Their contribution: the
> out-of-sample predictive R² of a linear polygenic score can be computed from
> summary-level data alone… That work must be cited as the foundation of
> everything below.

The identity `(wᵀz)²/(wᵀDw)` with an external summary-statistic vector and a
reference-panel LD matrix is older than that:

- **lassosum pseudovalidation** (Mak, Porsch, Choi, Zhou & Sham 2017, *Genet
  Epidemiol* 41:469) selects the penalty by maximizing `βᵀr/√(βᵀRβ)` — exactly
  the square root of PPB's estimator, with `r` from external summary statistics
  and `R` from a reference panel.
- **Quasi-correlation** (Pattee & Pan 2020, *PLOS Comput Biol* 16:e1008271) is
  the same quantity presented explicitly as *"a model-fitting metric that can be
  used to evaluate the performance of a polygenic risk score on out-of-sample
  data"* which *"estimates the true correlation"* — i.e. PPB's estimand, named
  and published two years before the preprint.

`FINISHING_PLAN.md` already lists both under *"Summary-statistic PRS evaluation
(same estimator family)"*, so the project knows. The problem is that the
**method and claims documents do not** — `METHOD.md` §1 and `CROSS_ANCESTRY.md`
both present the identity as the source paper's contribution. For a repository
whose stewardship section is otherwise scrupulous about who gets credit, that is
an inconsistency worth fixing before any manuscript.

This does not diminish the project; it relocates the contribution. The defensible
delta is:

1. Witteveen et al.'s contribution is the **shared public artifact** — a released
   LD + summary-statistic bundle that lets independent groups score PGS methods on
   a common target without individual-level data — not the estimator.
2. PPB's contribution is (a) the **cross-ancestry framing and its failure-mode
   map**, (b) the **infrastructure** (int8 block LD store, harmonization,
   provenance-checked results registry), and (c) the **discipline** — exact joint
   support, fail-closed overlap correction, declared gauge.

Claiming (1)–(3) is stronger and more accurate than claiming the identity.
Recommend also checking prior art for the cross-ancestry *application* before
"new to this project" is repeated in a manuscript: cross-population construction
methods (PRS-CSx, CT-SLEB, MUSSEL) tune on target-ancestry data, and some do so
with summary-statistic criteria of this family.

**Fix:** add a "prior art" paragraph to `METHOD.md` §1 and `CROSS_ANCESTRY.md`
naming quasi-correlation and pseudovalidation; restate the novelty claim as
benchmark + cross-ancestry characterisation.

### I2 (major) — the metric has a closed-form maximizer on the published bundle

`R² = (wᵀz)²/(wᵀDw)` is maximized at `w ∝ D⁻¹z`, giving `zᵀD⁻¹z`. Everything
needed is *in the published bundle*. `docs/TRANSFERABILITY.md` notes the
"refit collapse" and Gate D says released target statistics are "effectively
public labels", but neither quantifies what that means for a leaderboard.

Measured (Balding-Nichols B target, r_g = 1 so the honest score is at its best;
attacker solves `(D_ref + λI)⁻¹ z_B` on the released panel and released `z`;
"true R²" is individual-level in a fresh 20k B cohort):

| m | N_target | m/N | honest reports | honest true | attack reports | attack true | metric gain |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1000 | 20000 | 0.05 | 0.4112 | 0.3946 | 0.5945 | 0.4342 | **1.45×** |
| 1000 | 4000 | 0.25 | 0.3586 | 0.3872 | 0.7724 | 0.3155 | **2.15×** |
| 1000 | 2000 | 0.50 | 0.4096 | 0.4040 | 1.0612 | 0.2434 | **2.59×** |
| 2000 | 2000 | 1.00 | 0.3767 | 0.3923 | 1.6715 | 0.1492 | **4.44×** |

Two things matter. First, the attack needs no cleverness — it is one linear
solve. Second, `λ` is a **plausibility dial**: at λ = 1 the same attack reports
1.35× the honest score while staying well below 1, so the "R² > 1 means an
assumption failed" diagnostic in `LIMITATIONS.md` does **not** catch a tuned
submission. PPB's real regime is `m = 1,444,196` against target GWAS of
N ≈ 10⁵, i.e. `m/N ≈ 6–14` — off the bottom of this table.

This is a design-forward warning, not a live problem: the stage-1 leaderboard is
maintainer-run baselines with no submission path. But it means the hidden-test
track is not a nice-to-have for v1.0, and the current leaderboard page should say
so next to the table rather than only in the plan.

**Fix:** state the closed-form exploit in `results/schema.md` and on the rendered
leaderboard; make the hidden-target-`z` requirement explicit in Gate D rather
than the softer "public labels" wording.

### I3 (medium) — "+58% at r_g = 0.8" is arithmetic, not a measurement

The README, `FINISHING_PLAN.md`, and `CROSS_ANCESTRY.md` all headline the
discovery-substitution failure at **+58%**. Reproduced exactly (+57.668%). But
substituting `z_A` estimates `R²_A`, so the number is
`R²_A/R²_B − 1 = 1/0.648 − 1 = +54.3%`, plus the 3% LD term — it is *fully
determined by the r_g and F_ST chosen for the simulation*. Choose r_g = 0.5 and
it becomes several hundred percent.

It is a correct illustration of a real point (target-ancestry phenotype data is
irreducible) and the docs do say "in simulation". But quoted as a bare headline
it reads as a measured effect size. Recommend quoting it as
"≈ 1/portability − 1, so +58% at the demo's r_g = 0.8" wherever it appears.

## Theory and validation

### T1 (major) — the cross-ancestry simulation cannot produce LD-driven portability loss

In `src/ppb/simulate.py::_diploid_dosages` the latent haplotype correlation is
`rho ** |i-j|` — **the same matrix for both populations**. Only the MAF-implied
thresholds `_norm_ppf(1 - maf)` differ. Balding-Nichols therefore gives the two
ancestries different allele frequencies over an *identical LD architecture*.

The consequence is visible in the shipped output and is not commented on
anywhere: at **r_g = 1.0**, with F_ST = 0.25, portability is **0.998**. In this
simulation, ancestry divergence by itself costs nothing; *all* portability loss
comes from the `r_g` knob.

Real portability loss is driven substantially by LD and MAF divergence at
`r_g ≈ 1` (Wang et al. 2020; Ding et al. 2023 — both already in the reference
list). So `experiments/cross_ancestry.py` validates the estimator's algebra —
which was never in doubt, it is an identity — but does **not** exercise it under
the mechanism the project exists to measure. `FINISHING_PLAN.md` Phase 3 item 2
("recovery of the portability loss as a function of r_g and F_ST") cannot be
satisfied by this generator, because F_ST has no LD channel in it.

**Fix:** give the two populations different LD. Cheapest credible options, in
order of effort: (a) different `rho` per population; (b) different block
boundaries per population (recombination-history divergence); (c) draw haplotypes
from an actual coalescent with population split (`msprime`) for the Phase-3
validation. Also make the causal effects per-allele rather than per-standardized
genotype so MAF divergence changes the standardized effect sizes.

### T2 (major) — the −3% wrong-ancestry-LD bias is a property of the generator, not a transferable number

`docs/CROSS_ANCESTRY.md` reports mismatched-LD bias of **−3.02%**, and
`FINISHING_PLAN.md` calls LD mismatch "the dominant cross-ancestry error". Those
two statements are in tension, and the reason is the simulation's LD strength.

Sweeping the shipped harness (`run(m=1000, block_size=100, t_crit=0.0,
n_causal=100, n_phenos=25, seed=1)`, varying `rho`):

| within-block AR(1) ρ | mismatch-A % bias | independent-B % bias | LD-form ratio |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.5 (**shipped**) | −3.17 | +0.06 | 1.034 |
| 0.8 | −10.66 | −0.77 | 1.113 |
| 0.9 | −13.70 | −0.61 | 1.154 |

Score density and dimension barely move it (t_crit 4.0 → 0.0 at m = 500:
−2.52% → −2.24%; m = 500/1000/2000 dense: −2.24/−3.17/−2.25%). **LD strength
dominates.** AR(1) at ρ = 0.5 decays to r = 0.06 within four variants; the
shipped HM3+ reference has blocks of median 1,901 variants (max 17,304) with LD
extending far further than that. The −3% is therefore a lower bound of unknown
tightness, and should not be quoted as the magnitude of the mismatch failure
mode.

**Fix:** either widen the reported figure to a ρ-indexed range with the caveat,
or re-derive it on a realistic LD panel (the shipped bigsnpr reference is right
there — measure `wᵀD_A w / wᵀD_B w` for a real PGS across two real ancestry
panels as soon as a second panel exists).

### T3 (medium) — R² is quadratically sensitive to the scale of `z`, and nothing checks it

If `z → c·z` then `R² → c²·R²`. Unlike a rescale of `w`, this does **not** cancel
— `z` appears only in the numerator. And the scale of `z` is set entirely by the
reported `(beta, se, n)` triple that `ppb.standardized_marginal` consumes:

- **genomic control** (applied by GIANT and GLGC, at study *and* meta level)
  inflates `se`, deflating `z` and therefore `R²` by roughly the inflation factor;
- **meta-analysis SE handling**, SPA/SAIGE standard errors for binary traits, and
  any non-linear-model `se` shift it;
- **the analyst's choice of `n_eff`** shifts it directly.

The project already has the evidence in its own documents: `docs/REAL_DATA.md`
notes that DIAGRAM T2D at n_eff = 88,810 versus Privé's 72,100 "rescales the
statistic by a constant ~1.2×" — and the headline is reported to three
significant figures. That is the same sensitivity, from the mildest of the three
sources.

`LIMITATIONS.md` has a "gauge (standardization) consistency" entry, but it is
about `w`; there is no entry for the scale of `z`, and no diagnostic anywhere in
the pipeline.

**Fix:** add a limitation entry; add a cheap scale diagnostic to the real-data
path — mean χ² against the LD-score expectation, or the LDSC intercept if
available, or at minimum record whether the source study applied genomic control
in `target.*` provenance so a reader can discount accordingly.

### T4 (medium) — spec and real-data path disagree on covariate adjustment

`METHOD.md` §4 specifies forming **both** `z` and `D` from PC-adjusted genotypes
("Forming `z` and `D` from the PC-adjusted genotypes/phenotype removes
population-structure confounding"). The real-data path pairs a `z` that is a
*partial* correlation (real GWAS adjust for age, sex, PCs, centre) with an
**unadjusted** bigsnpr LD matrix. Within a homogeneous EUR sample the discrepancy
is small — but it is exactly the assumption the flagship cross-ancestry
application will lean on hardest, in structured targets where adjustment matters
most.

Related and smaller: `standardized_marginal` uses `r = t/√(t² + n − 2)`, the
no-covariate degrees of freedom. With `k` covariates it is `n − k − 2`;
immaterial at GWAS N, but worth one sentence in the docstring since the module
is explicitly about honouring per-variant `n`.

**Fix:** state the adjusted-`z`/unadjusted-`D` pairing in `REAL_DATA.md`'s
caveats, and either relax `METHOD.md` §4 to describe what is actually done or
carry a PC-adjusted LD reference.

### T6 (medium) — meta-analysis targets have no single generating population

For GIANT height the per-variant N runs 50.0k–253k across ~80 contributing
cohorts. Each `z_j` is then a marginal correlation in a *different* mixture of
cohorts, while `D` is one panel's LD. The registry records the N distribution
honestly (`n_eff_basis`, `n_eff_range`) — good — but neither `REAL_DATA.md` nor
`LIMITATIONS.md` names the consequence: the estimand is only approximately "the
squared correlation in a population", because there is no single population.
This also interacts with T3, since different cohorts contribute different amounts
of genomic-control deflation.

**Fix:** one paragraph in `LIMITATIONS.md`.

### T5 (verified, no action) — the finite-sample bias constant is right

`FINISHING_PLAN.md` plans to subtract `wᵀD_Bw/N_B` and report
`SE(R²) ≈ 2√(R²/N_B)`. Monte-Carlo check (4,000 resamples from a 200,000-person
synthetic population, m = 200, two heritabilities, two N):
`(E[(wᵀẑ)²] − (wᵀz)²) / (wᵀDw/N)` = 0.84, 1.31, 1.07, 1.07 — consistent with the
documented constant of 1. The planned correction is correct as specified.

## Implementation

### C1 (minor, corrected) — `ppb evaluate` strand-resolves the bundle against itself

> **Correction.** This was first written up as a **major** finding with a 33%
> understatement of R². **That severity was wrong.** The 0.100-vs-0.150 gap is
> caused by the *weights*-side palindrome drop, which is correct behaviour for an
> external submission — and because the evaluation uses the *intersection* of the
> two supports, retaining the bundle's own `z` at those variants recovers
> nothing. Verified directly: dropping on the weights side only, with `z` fully
> retained, still gives **0.100**. No CLI or published number is wrong. What
> remains is the reporting/efficiency wart below.

`evaluate()` harmonizes `sumstats_variants → ld_variants`
(`src/ppb/evaluate.py:58`). The CLI passes the bundle's own variant table for
both (`src/ppb/cli.py:22-26`), so `remove_ambiguous=True` runs strand resolution
on a table against itself — where there is no strand to resolve. Consequences:
`sumstats_report.n_ambiguous_removed` reports removals for a problem that does
not exist (20 of 60 in the fixture), and a full O(n) matching pass is spent
reproducing an identity mapping.

**Fix:** recognise the self-match and skip the pass; keep resolving
`weights_variants`, which is a submission with a genuinely unknown strand.

### C2 (medium) — no per-block sign check in `BlockDiagonalLD.quad`

`ld_backend.py:130-135` sums `backend.quad(...)` over blocks and returns the
total; `estimator.r2` rejects only a non-positive **total**. D8 blocks carry no
PSD certificate (`METHOD.md` says so). A single negative block would be masked by
430 positive ones, silently deflating the denominator and **inflating** the
headline R² — the one direction the project has been careful to fail closed on
everywhere else. The block level is where the failure signal actually is, and
`overlap.block_products` already materialises per-block values without checking
them either.

**Fix:** raise in `BlockDiagonalLD.quad` if any block returns < 0, naming the
block. Cheap, and it converts a silent inflation into a loud error.

### C3 (medium) — the PSD gate covers a small minority of shipped blocks

`ldref._PSD_CHECK_MAX = 512`, but the shipped reference's blocks are min 216,
**median 1,901**, max 17,304 (`METHOD.md` Table 1). So the exact eigen-check runs
on only the smallest tail, and the `-0.1` tolerance is a heuristic, not a bound
derived from int8 rounding. `LIMITATIONS.md`'s "checked where tractable"
understates how little is covered.

**Fix:** λ_min does not need a cubic decomposition — a shifted power iteration or
Lanczos on `λ_max·I − D` costs O(m²·iters) and is entirely tractable at
m = 17,304. Run it at conversion time and store the per-block λ_min as an
annotation in the `.npz`, so the reader validates against a recorded value
instead of recomputing or skipping.

### C4 (minor) — `evaluate()` computes `wᵀDw` twice

`evaluate.py:84-85` calls `_r2` and `_mse`, each of which calls `ld.quad(w)`.
Confirmed by instrumenting the backend: one `evaluate()` on a single-block
reference invokes `quad` **2** times. Genome-wide that doubles the one expensive
operation.

**Fix:** compute `den = ld.quad(w)` once and pass it to both, or give the
estimator a `quad`-taking private form.

### C5 (minor) — `harmonize_to` is the sweep bottleneck and rebuilds its index per call

Pure-Python double loop with per-element `_norm_chrom` string work, and it
rebuilds the reference position index on **every** call. Measured: 0.45 s per
120k variants → ≈ **16 s** per genome-wide sweep at the three calls per
chromosome `regenerate_results.sweep` makes. That matches `METHOD.md`'s own
observation that harmonization, not the quadratic form, dominates the 38.6 s
sweep — so the packed-LD speedup it documents is being diluted by this.

**Fix:** vectorise with `np.lexsort` + `searchsorted` on normalised
`(chrom, pos)`, or at minimum cache the reference index (it is rebuilt
identically three times per chromosome today).

### C6 (minor) — unguarded `af` use in the real-data sweep

`scripts/regenerate_results.py:205` reads `ref["af"]` unconditionally
(`read_ldref` only returns it when present → `KeyError` on a reference without
frequencies) and forms `sd = √(2f(1−f))` with no guard against `f ∈ {0, 1}`,
which would silently zero those weights rather than reporting them.

### C7 (minor) — MSE is emitted unconditionally

`EvaluationResult` and the CLI always report MSE. `docs/REAL_DATA.md` states the
real-data weight scale is arbitrary ("raw-unit (e.g. cm) weights are fine for R²,
not for MSE"), so the emitted MSE is meaningless on the project's own primary
path. Suppress it, or flag it as valid only under a declared absolute weight scale.

### Test-suite note (no action)

45 of the 45 skips are in `tests/test_results_registry.py`, all conditional on
registry content ("no current fit recorded", "fit is not correctable", "no legacy
diagnostic recorded"). So the registry-side rules for a *correctable* record are
never exercised by a committed pack. The unit-level correctable path **is**
covered (`tests/test_overlap.py`, two tests asserting
`fit.status == "correctable"`), so this is a coverage observation, not a gap —
worth a synthetic fixture pack if a correctable record is ever expected.

## Verified sound

Re-derived or re-measured independently; no action needed.

- **Core identity and MSE identity.** `corr(Xw, y)² = (wᵀz)²/(wᵀDw)` for
  standardized `y`; `MSE = var_y − 2wᵀz + wᵀDw`. `estimator.py` implements
  exactly this and refuses non-finite or non-positive denominators rather than
  clamping.
- **Cross-ancestry algebra.** `r_g` has no slot (because `z_B` is measured in B
  and already carries B-LD-tagged effects) and `var(y_B)` cancels on the
  correlation scale. The "irreducible numerator" argument — `wᵀD_Bw` is
  computable from a genotype panel alone, `wᵀz_B` is not — is correct.
- **Gauge analysis.** Invariance to a global but not a per-variant rescale of `w`;
  the HWE `√(2p(1−p))` deviation in the current real-data path is correctly
  identified, correctly scoped to "acceptable for the EUR anchor, not for
  structured/admixed targets", and correctly listed as a prerequisite for the
  first real cross-ancestry run.
- **PUMAS moments.** `t_train | t ~ N((N_train/N)t, (N_train N_val/N)V)` gives
  `Var(wᵀz_val) = (N_train/(N·N_val))·(var_y·wᵀDw + (wᵀz)²)`, which is exactly
  `conditional_scale * moment_quad` in `pumas.py:257,281`. The `'auto'` policy
  (correct for independent weights, raw for fitted weights) is the right default
  and its approximation is stated.
- **Overlap model.** The generalized Deming objective in `_profile_eiv` is the
  correct functional-model criterion with known error variances (no missing
  log-determinant term). The fail-closed gate ladder, the refusal to use variant
  count as a fallback basis, the `basis_unavailable` verdict for final LDpred2
  weights, and the explicit statement that `gamma > 0` can reflect shared
  stratification rather than participant overlap are all right — and the decision
  to record a weak-identification boundary instead of declaring recovery is the
  most credible thing in the repository. **(Superseded — see the follow-up
  below: that boundary turned out to be a property of the simulation, and the
  method recovers once the design is identified.)**
- **LD store.** Packed-vs-square losslessness, the unit-diagonal enforcement at
  the backend boundary, LR8's PSD-by-construction guarantee and the honest
  measurement that it is only 1.34× better than the lossless triangle, the
  refusal to mirror ldpred3's shrinkage with a correct statement of which
  direction it would bias R² — all sound, and the int8 error analysis
  ("not common-mode, so it does not cancel; what saves it is magnitude") is
  unusually careful.
- **Registry and leaderboard.** Untrusted-input handling in
  `build_leaderboard.py` (escaping, `parse_constant` rejection of non-finite JSON,
  PGS-id regex before linking), the num/den significant-digit rule, the
  immutability rule with its one documented exception, and the display of
  in-sample rows as upper bounds.
- **Claims hygiene.** Measure-vs-predict, the narrow privacy wording, "validated
  in simulation, not yet run on real cross-ancestry data", the restricted-score
  caveat at 88.6–92.1% support, and "binary output is not liability-scale R²" are
  stated consistently in every document that makes the corresponding claim.
- **Reproducibility.** `experiments/cross_ancestry.py` reproduces the
  `CROSS_ANCESTRY.md` table exactly; the finite-sample bias constant checks out;
  no stale test counts remain in the documentation.

## Resolution (2026-07-25)

All findings addressed. Suite after the changes: **344 passed, 45 skipped,
1 xfailed** (337 + 7 new tests), runtime 49.9 s → 34.9 s.

### Code

- **C2** — `BlockDiagonalLD.quad` raises when any block's quadratic form is
  negative beyond float rounding, naming the block
  (`tests/test_ld_backend.py::test_block_diagonal_rejects_negative_block_quadratic_form`,
  which checks the *total* stays positive so a sum-only check would miss it, plus
  a companion test that rounding on a genuinely PSD block does not trip it).
- **C3** — added `min_eig_upper_bound()` (Lanczos with full reorthogonalization)

## Follow-up: uncertainty and negative controls (2026-07-25)

Two gaps the review surfaced but did not list as findings, since neither is a
defect — they are things a benchmark needs and did not have. Both are computed
from the per-block `u_b`, `v_b` the genome-wide sweep already builds, so neither
costs a second pass over the LD reference (`src/ppb/diagnostics.py`).

**Block jackknife SE** (`r2_block_jackknife`). `regenerate_results.sweep` was
building 431 per-block products per target and discarding everything but the two
sums. A delete-one-block (and delete-one-chromosome) jackknife now rides along.
Validated against the sampling variability it claims to estimate: over 300
replicates the reported SE is within 6% of the empirical SD. It also reports
`max_variance_share`, the largest single group's share of the jackknife
variance — the first attempt normalized the largest delete-one shift by the SE,
which a test showed detects nothing, because a dominating group inflates both.

**Block-sign-flip null** (`sign_flip_null`). `D` is block-diagonal, so negating
every weight in a block flips `u_b` and leaves `v_b` exactly unchanged (asserted
bit-for-bit against the real backend). The sign-flipped scores are therefore a
null family with the same denominator and the same per-block magnitudes, giving
an exact null with no permuted phenotype: `E[R²_null] = Σu_b²/Σv_b` and
`z = Σu_b/√(Σu_b²)`. Calibration checked over 300 replicates — under a null
score `z` has mean −0.08 and sd 0.93. `z` is bounded by `√n_blocks` (20.8 here),
so it is a coherence measure on a fixed scale, not an unbounded significance
statistic; that ceiling is documented and tested.

**Trait-swap matrix** (`scripts/negative_controls.py`). Evaluates every score
against every consortium target in one sweep. The genuinely correlated pairs
(BMI/T2D, LDL/CAD) are **declared in the script**, not rationalised after the
run, so the control cannot be quietly relaxed to accommodate its own output; an
unexplained off-diagonal exits non-zero.

Neither diagnostic detects a uniformly mis-scaled `z` (T3): observed and null
move together. Stated in the code, the schema, and `LIMITATIONS.md`.

The registry schema carries all of it as optional fields, validated when present
— `baseline-2026-07.json` predates them and legitimately lacks them — with
round-trip tests proving what the generator emits passes the validators, and
that the validators reject malformed variants. Suite: **439 passed, 45 skipped,
1 xfailed**.

## Follow-up: the overlap correction is salvageable (2026-07-25)

The review's suggestion #6 was to decide `ppb.overlap`'s status — ~600 lines,
20 tests, and a method document for machinery that had never produced a
correction, whose own simulation was retained as a strict `xfail` "operating
boundary". Investigated; **it works, and the boundary was an artifact.**

**Diagnosis.** `fit_overlap` separates two design columns: the reference signal
`u_R` and the trainer basis `q`. The shipped simulation used a *marginal*
trainer (`w = z_train`, so `A = I`) over *equal-sized* blocks, which makes
`q_b = tr(A_b' K_b) = tr(D_b)` the block size — literally
`np.full(m_blocks, 50.0)`, the same number for every block. It also used a
*diffuse* architecture (20% of variants causal), which makes `u_R` near-constant
too. Two constant columns cannot be separated, so the gate refused. That was the
least identifiable configuration the harness can produce, and the refusal was
correct but uninformative. The estimate underneath was never far off: γ = 2.22e−4
against a truth of 2.50e−4, refused at VIF 2.3 against a threshold of 2.0.

**What decides identification** is signal variation *at fixed block size* — both
columns are positive and both grow with block size, so only the idiosyncratic
part separates them. Sweeping the two candidate knobs:

| blocks | causal fraction | VIF | status |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| equal (63 × 50) | 0.20 | 1.90 | mostly refused |
| equal (63 × 50) | 0.01 | 1.10 | **correctable** |
| heterogeneous (8× size range) | 0.20 | 1.86 | refused |
| heterogeneous (8× size range) | 0.01 | 1.16 | **correctable** |

Causal sparsity is the decisive knob, not block heterogeneity. Real polygenic
traits over 1.44M HM3+ variants are far sparser than 20% causal, so the
realistic regime is the identified one — an argument for expecting
identification on real data, not evidence of it.

**End-to-end result** with a rerunnable blockwise-ridge trainer, a Hutchinson
basis, and heterogeneous LD blocks:

| architecture | overlap | status | γ/true | R² naive | R² corrected | anchor |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| diffuse (20%) | 100% | `weak_identification` | 0.76 | — | refused | — |
| sparse (1%) | 0% | `not_detected` | — | 0.056 | none issued | 0.041 |
| sparse (1%) | 25% | `correctable` | 0.84 | 0.158 | **0.050** | 0.047 |
| sparse (1%) | 100% | `correctable` | 0.99 | 1.094 | **0.062** | 0.038 |

A statistic inflated nearly thirtyfold returns to within ~1.6× of its honest
anchor; partial overlap lands on it; the null issues nothing.

**The missing implementation.** `OVERLAP.md` Equation 4 — the stochastic
generalized-degrees-of-freedom basis that a rerunnable trainer needs — had never
been implemented, so even a trainer that *could* supply a basis had no way to.
`ppb.estimate_overlap_basis` now does, validated against the exact
`tr(A_b' K_b)` of a linear trainer (0.2–0.6% error). Two details were needed to
make it measure anything:

- **The step must be scaled to the data.** My first version perturbed by
  `delta * g` with `g ~ N(0, D)` — twenty times larger than `z` itself, which
  makes *any* trainer look like the identity. A thresholding trainer sailed
  through the stability gate reporting the basis of a linear one. Steps are now
  fractions of `‖z‖`.
- **One small step is not a stability check.** A hard-thresholding trainer has a
  locally *constant* Jacobian (its selection matrix), so it looks perfectly
  stable near `z` while missing that selection itself responds to the shared
  noise. The default steps span from too small to move any boundary to large
  enough to move many; a p+T trainer is now refused on that disagreement, and on
  the per-block pattern correlation the total spread alone missed.

**Verdict: keep it.** The strict `xfail` is replaced by six passing tests, and
`OVERLAP.md`, `FINISHING_PLAN.md`, `LIMITATIONS.md`, and both READMEs no longer
report the refusal as the method's limit. It remains simulation-only — no real
score has supplied a basis, the correction is an upper bound rather than an
unbiased estimate, and a sufficiently diffuse trait is still refused outright.
Suite: **450 passed, 45 skipped** (no xfail).
